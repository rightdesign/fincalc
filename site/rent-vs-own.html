Rent vs own

Compare buying a home with renting a similar one and investing the difference. Enter the home price and today's rent; the calculator shows which leaves you with more money after the years you plan to stay.

Methodology and assumptions
  • Owning: you pay the down payment and closing costs, then the monthly mortgage payment (a standard fixed-rate loan) plus property tax, insurance and maintenance, each a percentage of the home's value that year. The home's value grows at the rate you enter. When you sell, selling costs are deducted and the loan is repaid. Your net worth is what is left.
  • Renting: you pay rent (rising once a year by the rent growth) plus renter's insurance, and invest the down payment and closing costs you did not spend. Each month, whatever owning costs beyond renting is also invested; if renting costs more, the shortfall is taken from the investments. Investments grow at the return you enter. Your net worth is the investment balance.
  • Break-even is the first year after which owning stays ahead through the end of your stay.
  • Mortgage insurance (PMI) is a percentage of the original loan per year, charged each month while the loan balance is above 80% of the home’s value (so it ends as you pay down and the home appreciates) and counted in the monthly cost of owning. Not modelled: income tax effects (the mortgage interest and property tax deductions, taxes on investment gains), variable rates (see the variable-rate mortgage calculator), and inflation. The investment return is a steady assumption, not a forecast.

Not financial advice. This calculator gives estimates based on the inputs and simplifications described above. Real costs and returns differ; talk to a qualified professional before buying a home.
